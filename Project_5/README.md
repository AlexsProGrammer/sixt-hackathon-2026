# Project 5: Meta-Evaluation Framework for LLM-as-Judge Agentic Systems

**Project owner:** Jigyasa Singhal

**Questions before the hackathon:** email the project owner directly: jigyasa.singhal@sixt.com. On the day, ask on site.

## Problem
An AI model checks the replies written by another AI model. If the AI judge says a reply is good, it is sent to the customer automatically. But nobody has really verified whether this judge is reliable. Recent 2026 research found that many AI judges look reliable on the surface but are not. A judge can agree with itself every time on the same case and still be consistently unfair or biased in one direction. A judge can look consistent and still be wrong.

## Approach
- We provide test cases: good replies with **harmless** edits (filler, polite opener, translation) and **harmful** edits ("may" becomes "will", a removed condition, a wrong number or policy detail).
- Design a step-by-step method to improve the judge: find failures, move checkable facts to rule-based code, improve the AI judge, and check repeat consistency and agreement with humans (Cohen's kappa), in EN / DE / FR / ES / IT.
- At the end, apply your method **unchanged** to a second judge you have not seen.
- Submit code. It is scored on a hidden test set.

## Data
Public data only: the Bitext customer-support dataset, translated into 5 languages, plus a fictional company policy. Test cases are generated by the organizers. A starter toolkit and a baseline judge are provided.

## Expected outcome
- A reusable, step-by-step method to make any AI judge more trustworthy.
- Before/after results on two judges, by edit type and language.
- A working rules-plus-AI judge.
- Insights into why judges fail.

**Results are judged on:** score on the hidden test set (both judges), kappa and consistency before/after, and how reusable your method is.
